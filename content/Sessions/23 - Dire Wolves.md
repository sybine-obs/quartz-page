## North by NorthEast - rolling hills and waymarkers

![[Images/Travel Maps/Elven waystation on the way north.png#edge|cover circle hsmall left lp]]<span style="font-style:italic;font-weight:bold" class="hide-data" data-calendar="Faerun Calendar" data-date="1502-Hammer-17" data-end="" data-name="Continuing Northeast" data-category="Character Journal">Hammer 17 - Continuing Northeast</span>
Thunderheads crowded the sky in the morning, sparking with a bit of lightning. It was a dramatic, but blew by us to the north. The lightning did illuminate the terrain a bit as we head out, making the way easier.

We wound through the deeper drifts, seeking the more secure footing of ridges and rocky ground.

The hills opened up into what would have been grassland, but in this season was rolling and deceptive mounds of snow. We crossed a small stream that ran along our path for a short distance that could be a tributary filling the lake of the quarry. The very tips of tall grass poked out of some of the drifts, and it must have been thick and tall in the fall. At one point we crossed man-made irrigation channels. This area might have once been farmed. It could be again.

We crunched along through the snow and over the stone foundation of a long abandoned farmstead. There was evidence of a wild field and of old bits of corn and other plants. In one spot it had been dug down to bare earth and torn up. The old tracks around it were that of a large wild boar. Another opportunity to hunt.

Pressing north _(into the next hex)_ we started cross between some small ridges, but nothing high enough to block the view. Here we crossed tracks from a small herd of snow elk, traveling generally to the east and a circle of weathered standing stone clearly hundreds of years old. It was not a temple or ruined habitation but rather a waymarker. Doran dug one out and it carried a simple carved arrow pointing to the northeast. It looks like we will follow its direction in the morning.

Resting by the stone was uneventful.

## Bitter cold

<span style="font-style:italic;font-weight:bold" class="hide-data" data-calendar="Faerun Calendar" data-date="1502-Hammer-18" data-end="" data-name="Continuing Northeast" data-category="Character Journal">Hammer 18 - Biting, bitter winds</span>
Druidcraft for the day reveals: bitter northerly wind. _(Extreme cold rules will apply. Cold weather gear gives advantage.)_

With Phelan's warning of the weather to come, we did our best to prepare for the cold. His poor Mule, Doug, was well into his shaggy winter coat, but that would not be enough. It took some careful coaxing, but we draped him in blankets and added the fabric of one of our tents to try and cut the biting wind.

Even with that preparation, the cold clearly still wore upon them. While most of us were warmed enough by the exertion of walking, Phelan and Doug both seemed sapped by the cold _(one level exhaustion)_ as we made our way north and started to climb the craggy rocks of the early hills.
Between the hills, the drifts were high enough to slow movement. Something about the rock here seems older than the surrounding country. If only the snow were gone, that we might take more time to explore it.

We climbed one last hill and decided to take shelter on its slope, as much out of the wind as possible. But the view from the crest would possibly reveal much about the surrounding land. I drew upon the new form we saw while following the Winter Wolves -- and there is a special joy in the shape of a snow martin. I was able to bound up, warm in its fur, and climb to the peak for a good look. Sadly, the light had already left the sky and there was no chance of spotting columns of smoke. No fires marked the lands beyond. Deeper shadows outlined what might be a valley to the north. The longer I looked, the more shapes I could pick out of the darkness. In the deepness of the shadow below there could have been some twisted trees along a stream with just the faintest pinpricks of reflected light. Long draws cut by rockslides. One rockslide was broken by a wider shelf, and something there. Half-hidden. Faint, irregular darkening in the snow. Could be a collapsed structure. Could be a camp. Its sharp and regular lines were clearly not of natural origin.
To the north, there was nothing of note, just the empty northern horizon.

I took a moment there, curling up in the lea of a stone and listening to the wind scream by and watch for stars to wink in and out from behind the obscuring roll of clouds.

With one last look, I spotted a thin line that could be an old trail or cleared path running into a narrow cleft or cave mouth.
Further to the east a single dark shape that stood against the white. It was too big to be a person, too still to be someone walking. It finally clicked in my mind -- another standing stone, set so the long face pointed in the same direction we had been traveling, and the same as the waystones we had passed -- north-northeast.

I returned to camp.

## A choice - how far to go

<span style="font-style:italic;font-weight:bold" class="hide-data" data-calendar="Faerun Calendar" data-date="1502-Hammer-19" data-end="" data-name="Making a choice" data-category="Character Journal">Hammer 19 - Thick mist obscures the way</span>
We rested through another uneventful night, huddling out of the wind. In the morning, the hills were all shrouded in a thick mist. We would need to trek into the valley to see more.

It was a fraught decision. We needed to return. We wanted to find Doran's people.

We would descend to the stone and leave a mark there, hopefully for them to find, and then return our bounty to Snowmantle.
