# Regrouping with the Stonecrag clan

## Shelter and rest

<span style="font-style:italic;font-weight:bold" data-calendar="Faerun Calendar" data-date="1502-Hammer-22" data-name="Determining the way back to Snowmantle" data-category="Character Journal">~</span>
The sun rose with our party and the Goliaths gathered on the crest of the hill just south of where the Giants and Ogres laired. We considered our options of how to return with so many exhausted and hungry people with us. In the distance to the south and east, we could see the signs of a structure that might provide shelter for us to rest and also placed us in easy reach of the herd of winter elk we had seen signs of on our way here.
It was a unanimous decision for us to gamble on both shelter and food in one destination. After that, we could turn our path further south past the cache of food we'd left behind and on to Snowmantle.

We could not quite make it to the shelter before nightfall and we circled up to shelter through the darkest part of the night, watching the stars' slow march across the sky.

(Rest but not quite a SHORT REST - no exhaustion)

## Finding the temple

<span style="font-style:italic;font-weight:bold" data-calendar="Faerun Calendar" data-date="1502-Hammer-23" data-name="The Temple and the Elk Herd" data-category="Character Journal">~</span>
![[Images/Misc/TempleOfDeneir.png#edge|cover circle left hsmall lp]]We approached the structure. The area in front of it was filled with a small lake or wide stream, frozen nearly solid. The walls were old and worn, and the building looked to have seen better days, but it was not entirely decrepit. We split up -- Berrus, Doran and Heswyn began to scout the ruins, looking for signs of active magic. Bracken and Tinker flew along with.
There was writing on the door. Hank watched through Tinker's eyes and tried to decipher the writings. Berrus approached the door, and words formed before him in the air. We tried again, and through trial-and-error found they would reappear again after a time when someone approached, but the words were different.

> [!warning|color-red background-yellow] _"Only the hand that remembers the vow may pass"_

We worried that this was a passphrase that we did not understand, and that activating the door again might trigger a trap. But in the end we opted to try one more time. The words this time were different, again.

> [!warning|color-red background-yellow] _"Speak no name the mountain has forgotten"_

Hank read it and tried to explain it, and as he did he lifted a hand to cast an illusion of the words he had read ... and the door opened.

## Inside the temple

We began to explore the interior. Dorran blocked the doors, just to be careful. Within was a room a bit worse for wear with the passage of time. But many, many books filled stone shelves, some spilled across the floor where the shelves had given way. It was not a large room, but could provide us some shelter from the elements. There was a stairway that wound up to a second floor and down into a lower level as well.

Berrus lead the way, exploring. **He found a book marked with a date -- 1478**. Not so terribly long ago.

![[Images/Misc/Deneir.png#edge|cover right hsmall circle lp]]Several parts of the temple were marked with an unfamiliar sigil.

The stairs up lead into an open chamber where Doran found an unfinished circle on the floor. Around its circumference were placed five tomes, and in the center a stone lectern that stood empty. Doran saw this, and called for Hank to take a better look at such an obviously magical setup. Hank joined him upstairs and investigated the books and the circle (although at one point Tinker balked at being used as merely a tool to avoid risk). He entered the circle and picked up a book -- and the circle activated. The backlash as it unraveled rocked Hank, but did not seriously injure him. He left the circle for further exploration.

![[Images/Battle maps/TempleBasement.png#edge|cover circle left hsmall]]The stairs down lead into a larger chamber with bare walls. There were a few desks with papers still held down on their surface and a series of glyphs on the floor. Berrus called Hank in to take a look at them, and he sent Tinker, but they did not immediately resolve into something obvious. The sigils were magical in nature.
We gathered with Berrus even as he became impatient with waiting and charged through the symbols on the floor. They activated and threw him violently across the room, battering him badly and leaving him nearly unconscious. That almost had us all leaving the space alone entirely, but we needed safe grounds for the Goliath people to rest and the upper floor would not shelter us all. I began to look for a key to the sigils, and found writing on one of the desks.

## The Basement and the Blank Scroll

> [!abstract|color-red background-yellow] The First Clue
> _What has not yet been written cannot be judged._
> _The eye opens only on what the hand has not yet touched._
> _Light is given to what has already been sealed,_
> _never to the page that still waits._

It was a hint, but not enough to decipher the sigil's use. We looked further and found more...

> [!abstract|color-red background-yellow] The Second Clue
> _The hand moves before the book is closed._
> _The book is closed before the candle is lit._
> _The eye does not wait for either._
> _Nothing is recorded until the page is chosen._

And Hank found a third bit, scrawled in the margin of one of the open books..

> [!abstract|color-red background-yellow] The Third Clue (scrawled in a margin)
> _Begin with the absence of words._
> _The eye must see before the quill can mark._
> _What is finished is not yet safe._
> _Only after it is sealed does the light remain._

Armed with these fragments we debated and decided the order would be:
Blank page, Eye, Quill, Book, Candle

![[Images/Misc/ScrollOfKnowledge.png#edge|cover right hsmall lp]]And with a grinding sound, the back wall of the room slid open to reveal an alcove with a scroll case and a small piece of mirror. The case was inscribed with a prayer and as Hank read it, he revealed that this was a temple to Deneir, the god of glyphs and writing.

Hank lifted it out and examined the case briefly before opening it. Within was an old scroll and when opened, appeared to be blank. But I had a thought -- why would a temple to Deneir so carefully store a blank scroll, and what was the purpose of the mirror? I urged Hank to read the scroll looking in the mirror's reflection... and it revealed writing!

The writing, Hank explained later, was a fragment akin to the Metatext -- a mystical text that contained pieces of the foundation of magic itself. In reading it, he gained knowledge of spellcraft _(two new spells)_ and of the purpose and workings of this very temple. He knew how to work the spell circle above, if not its true purpose.

The glyphs were now inactive until we reset the small vault they had concealed. We began to move everyone inside to allow them to warm up and rest. I found a quiet space and drew carefully on the magic of the sleeping earth and cast my thoughts out to find where the elk might be running nearest our current location. One caught my consciousness to the south at the edge of the spell's range, implying there might be more to be found further in that direction.

## Spending the day -- hunting and (information) gathering

We settled in as best we were able, making the space warm and safe, securing most of the Goliaths there. We took those that were strong enough to hunt to the south, sharing out what weapons we had among us. While we did not find the main herd, we were able to fell nine elk. The Goliaths were able to help us return them to the temple to feed all our people for several days.

While some of us hunted, Hank further explored the temple and Phelan spoke with the Goliaths. Heswyn provided wood for the fires. Hank eventually returned to the upper chamber and began to look for the key to the spell circle -- a word that was missing from each of the five texts stored there.

> [!abstract|color-red background-yellow]- The First Text
> _The old maps of the northern hills were drawn with great care. Every ridge, every stream, and every stand of timber was set down so that later travelers would not be lost. Yet the cartographers who worked these lands left one thing unmarked on every sheet._
> _They recorded the paths men walked and the places men claimed, but they did not record the stillness that remained when the work was finished. In the space between the last line and the edge of the page, **(\__blank\__)** was allowed to stand. What is never drawn cannot be disputed, and what is never claimed cannot be taken._

> [!abstract|color-red background-yellow]- The Second Text
> The circle was prepared according to the old forms. The names of the winds were spoken, the offerings were placed, and the final invocation was written in full. When the storm did not answer, the scribes reviewed every word they had used and found no error in the text.
> They later noted that the ritual had failed not because a word was wrong, but because a word was missing. After the last spoken phrase there should have been a pause. That pause was never marked. The record simply ends. The missing measure is called **(\__blank\__)**.

> [!abstract|color-red background-yellow]- The Third Text
> Certain works were copied only in part. The scribes would reproduce the history, the names, and the methods, then deliberately omit the final instruction that would allow the knowledge to be used. They believed this protected both the reader and the work itself.
> The omitted portion was never replaced with another phrase. It was left as an absence. The vault still recognizes that absence. It listens for the thing that was withheld on purpose. It listens for **(\__blank\__)**.

> [!abstract|color-red background-yellow]- The Fourth Text
> Every book in the temple was examined for traces of the greater work. Words were compared, phrases were aligned, and repetitions were noted. The scribes eventually concluded that the Metatext could not be assembled from what had been written.
> It could only be approached by recognizing what every careful hand had chosen to leave out. Across centuries of copying, one choice remained consistent. They left only **(\__blank\__)**. The rest of the page was filled. That single refusal was never explained.

> [!abstract|color-red background-yellow]- The Fifth Text
> Passage 5 ( final notes of the temple’s last high scribe ) I have copied what I was given and sealed what I was told to seal. The circle above remains open because we were instructed never to complete it with ink. The last high librarian before me wrote that the work finishes itself only when the one word we were forbidden to record is finally acknowledged aloud.
> We were not to write it. We were not to teach it. We were only to leave the space. I have left the space. If any come after us who understand why the space was left, they will know to speak **(\__blank\__)**. Only then does the circle close, and only then is the record whole.

> [!info|color-red background-yellow]- Phelan's discussion with the Goliaths
> The Orc tribes had become much more aggressive and antagonistic. There was debate amongst the Goliaths if they should leave or go. There was contention amongst their tribe between the two options, some wanting to hold onto their strong roots in their home, and Sava Stonebind advocating to search to the south for a safer location. Old Keth originally opposed the decision to leave, but was finally convinced that they should move on. Most of those that stayed behind were older, those that would normally be left behind as they became less able to contribute to the well-being of the tribe.
> Phelan set about making them comfortable and explaining the situation we are currently in -- the harshness of the strange winter storm and our struggles with finding food. His mention of the abandoned quarry drew out strong interest -- for working stone is fundamental to the Goliath culture and they seemed reluctant to simply move in and integrate with the Human population of Snowmantle.

The day drew to a close as the hunting party returned with their bounty and everyone came together to cook, eat, and share.

## Closing the Circle

<span style="font-style:italic;font-weight:bold" data-calendar="Faerun Calendar" data-date="1502-Hammer-24" data-name="The missing word - Silence" data-category="Character Journal">~</span>

In the morning, Hank returned to the circle and activated it again with the intention of completing the spellwork. He gave it the final word: Silence.
The magic pooled within the circle, awakening the latent energies that had been stored in Hank's pipe, and leaving behind a piece of knowledge as if he had read an item unwritten:

> [!important|color-red background-yellow] The Unseen Journal Entry
> _Late autumn, 1478 DR_
> The old seals beneath Yûlash still hold, but the land around them does not forget. I have walked the northern ridges these past three seasons. In the low places between the hills the earth has gone soft and black where nothing should rot so cleanly. Trees lean and weep a slow decay from their hearts. Streams that once ran clear now carry the scent of turned graves, and the banks crumble where the water touches them. These are not the works of a living god. Moander was ended in the Year of Shadows. Finder took what remained of him, and the Darkbringer has not walked since. What lingers is only the echo—old seeds that never fully died, corruption that settled into soil and stone and has been waiting, patient, for something else to give it purpose.
> There is another presence along the ridges. I have not seen its face. Only the place where it has stood. A stillness that falls so complete the wind itself forgets to move.
> Once, at dusk, I found a single cut cord of braided hair lying on the path—cleanly severed, the ends already blackening as if the life had been drawn out of them rather than merely ended. No tracks led to it. No tracks led away. The land has begun to measure what it will keep and what it will release. The rot is old. The measuring is new. Something patient is already counting the vows that still hold, and the ones that have grown thin enough to part. I leave this where it may still be read by those who come after. The north is listening. It has not yet decided which of us it will allow to finish our seasons.

As Hank finished with the circle, we gathered the Goliaths, ourselves, and the food on makeshift travois, and turned towards Snowmantle. We would pick up our remaining cache of food on the way. Soon, we would see how the settlement had fared in our unexpectedly long absence.
