## A new arrival

Finn came running far too early in the morning with  a warning -- someone was approaching on the road. It took a little bit to rouse everyone to go see, although Doran was immediately out the door, and Berrus was so deeply asleep, we decided to leave him there for the time being. ![[Images/Misc/Fuzzy Caterpillar - mustache.png#edge|cover circle wsmall right lp]] (I only left a small gift to show who much I was thinking of him...)
We were warry to start, and Doran challenged the stranger, but the old human seemed friendly enough. He introduced himself as [[Hank]] Houdini, a mage. He was made aware of Jerana's endeavor by his school, and by Lord Morn in Dagger Falls. Apparently our trail here was easy enough to follow and after a bit we invited him inside the camp to meet Jerana.

## A rooftop moment

![[Images/Banners/Moonpetal Flowers.png#edge|cover circle left wmedium lp]]When exploring on my own, I had found an odd alcove off the upper floor of the temple, and it led to the roof giving me an idea as the moon waned in the sky. I gathered up the others to show them the way.
The stars were so bright and near this far out from any great city. And even the torches that might dazzle the eyes and dim their light were far away when we all emerged on the roof of the temple. The moon had hidden her face, leaving the constellations to blaze across the sky in all their glory. It was a moment we could all take for ourselves, even Hank who was so new to us all. He seemed to slide right into empty space Brendor had left behind, although I wonder if that might not be as comfortable to fill, or as easy, as it appeared.
I felt the call of the stars stronger each day. I could hear that silvery song echoing from the first moment when we encountered the great Orrery underneath White Crag. It filled the town now, as it was, still broken and wild. I wanted them to see that, before we could wall it in and fill it with our own light, dimming the sky's glory and taming the wild that had crept into this place in the last two hundred years.

None of them are elves. None will live the decades or centuries to see this settlement grow. It is important that they see what it is now, and understand what it might become. It is important to take the fleeting moments we are given and honor them for what they are.

Before we left, Doran tentatively caught my arm, and spoke of the stars to the north, his sky filled with constellations different than our own. He wants to show them to me. I think I would like that.

## A flickering light

Before we descended, one last thing caught my eye. A light flickered on a ridge to the east. I asked Aiwë to go see what it might be and return. It took some small time, but she flew back with tidings that set a chill that had nothing to do with the turning weather.\
A cloaked figure had stood upon that ridge, and as he watched the town, a light answered in a quick, staccato flicker. The figure took out a device and signaled in return before turning to follow the ridge to the north.
I told the others, and we all agreed this was a sign of something afoot. But what?

## The storm rolls in

The morning dawned bright and clear, with only a small threat of rain in Phelan's forecasting. It did not stay that way for long. Clouds gathered in the northwest, darker and more foreboding than any we had seen thus far. Heswyn revealed then that he felt a chill and a deep sense that his efforts in the temple had not gone unnoticed. In fact, he had felt this certainty once before as we left the temple roof. The wind immediately began to pick up, bringing with it the bite of cold and the sharp smell of snow and ice.
We mobilized to get the settlers to safe shelter. There was a choice -- the buildings we had repaired, or the temple itself. Despite the settlers' trepidation and distrust of the building itself, we felt it was the most likely to weather what appeared to be an unnatural storm. Supplies were quickly shifted and everyone ushered into the temple. As we took count of who was there, Selene rushed up -- Garren had not returned from the forest.
I could not leave him out there, and prevailed upon the others to assist me in finding him. And only then as heads were counted more carefully was it revealed that Elara Fisher was also missing.
It was a desperate choice, and I fear we may have made the wrong one. But Elara was thought only to be "somewhere to the south, hunting", and Garren was to the east where we had carefully marked the woods for what could be cut. Doran, Phelan and I gathered what we thought was needed as swiftly as we were able, and headed east.
![[Images/Misc/Raging snowstorm.png#edge|cover circle wmedium center right lp]]
The storm was worse than I ever imagined. It screamed like a beast. The wind broke branches and threw debris at our backs. It clawed away even the memory of heat. We found Garren, but that was barely half the task. Throwing the half-frozen man on my equine back, we turned into the wind and tried to find our way back.

We fought the storm for what felt like hours, but it couldn't have been that long. Its fury would have been awe-inspiring, had it been natural. But the storm felt ... angry. Violent. It sapped our strength, and as we pressed onwards, I began to fear we would not be able to find the settlement and the safety of the temple again.

We looked up from our exhausted trudge and found we had drifted in to the curve of the river. Safety was just beyond. The cold had clawed a solid scum of ice over the surface of the river, but it looked dangerous. It would likely not hold Doran. I called on the energies sleeping under the surface and the plants rose sluggishly to my call -- entangling vines that spread just under the ice to hold it firm. Phelan fell, but was able to cross. I went next, and could not hold against the storm. The ice cracked and water seeped through it, over me, chilling. Freezing. I thought this could be the end.

Doran leapt into action, his furious bellow audible even over the screaming winds. He grabbed Gerran and jumped over the river, quickly reaching out to me with his pick and pulling me to the shore. We stumbled through the cold and fell through the door into the safety and the relative warmth of the temple walls.
